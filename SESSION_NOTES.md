#### §265.09 — Criminal use of a firearm in the first degree: 5-year consecutive enhancement missing
**Scope note:** in scope — offense-specific sentencing consequence that changes sentence output, same pattern as existing `armedFelonyOverride` / `domesticViolenceIndeterminate` exceptions.

Current state: §265.09 is in `lists.json` as a ClassB_Felony, in VFO_LIST.classB (correctly), and routes through the standard VFO sentencing path in `sentencing.py`. No reference to the §265.09(2) 5-year consecutive enhancement anywhere in the codebase or data.

**The enhancement (PL 265.09(2)):** when a person is convicted of §265.09 and displayed a loaded weapon (from which a shot readily capable of producing death/serious injury may be discharged) in furtherance of the commission of the crime, the court **shall** impose an additional consecutive 5-year sentence on top of the sentence for the underlying class B VFO. Four gates:
1. Conviction is specifically §265.09 (offense-specific).
2. Defendant displayed a loaded weapon in furtherance of the crime (case-fact).
3. Defendant's criminal liability for the weapon display is NOT based solely on the conduct of another per §20.00 (accomplice-only exclusion — case-fact).
4. Court does NOT find on the record that the additional consecutive sentence would be "unduly harsh" AND that not imposing it would be consistent with public safety and would not deprecate the seriousness of the crime (court-finding gate — same pattern as `armedFelonyOverride` mitigating-factor gate).

**Aggregate-minimum rule:** the 5-year consecutive term aggregates with the minimum term of the sentence on the underlying class B VFO. The aggregate becomes the new aggregate minimum; defendant must serve the entire aggregate minimum and is NOT eligible for parole or conditional release during that term. This parole-ineligibility consequence is a real sentence output change.

**2013 amendment context:** §265.09(2) previously applied only when the underlying class B VFO sentence was indeterminate. The 2013 amendment made it apply regardless of whether the underlying sentence is indeterminate or determinate. The aggregate-minimum language in the statute still refers to "the indeterminate sentence imposed on the underlying class B violent felony" — largely vestigial now that class B VFOs are sentenced to determinate terms (post-2019 §60.12 amendments), but the 2013 amendment's "regardless of indeterminate or determinate" intent should govern the implementation.

Financial obligations and collateral not gated for juvenile offenders.** The treatise explicitly states predicate felon statutes and monetary penalties "cannot be applied to juvenile offenders." Our `generate_all_financial` at `sentencing.py:1386` and `generate_collateral_requirements` at `sentencing.py:1402` are called unconditionally — no gate on `age_category == JUVENILE_OFFENDER`. A juvenile offender gets fines, mandatory surcharges, CVAF, DNA fees, sex offender fees, etc. emitted in the output. Fix: gate both calls on `case_facts.defendant_age_category != AgeCategory.JUVENILE_OFFENDER`, or emit a reduced set (the statute says monetary penalties don't apply, but verify whether DNA sample requirement still applies — that's a collateral requirement, not a monetary penalty).
- [ ] **#JO5. Predicate felon enhancements not gated for juvenile offenders.** The treatise says predicate felon statutes "cannot be applied to juvenile offenders." Our `_normalize_prior_status` at `sentencing.py:298-358` and the routing logic don't check age category — a juvenile offender with `prior_status == SECOND_FELONY_OFFENDER_70_06` would still get the §70.06 ranges if the §70.05 routing didn't fire first. Since §70.05 routing does fire for A-I/B/C/D, this is mostly a latent issue for class E/A-II juvenile offenders (who fall through to adult sentencing). Fix: gate predicate enhancements on `age_category != JUVENILE_OFFENDER`, or ensure the §70.05 routing catches all juvenile cases before the predicate logic runs.

**Low priority:**
- [ ] **#JO6. Class E / A-II juvenile offenders fall through to adult sentencing silently.** §70.05(2) only covers A-I, B, C, D. Our routing at `sentencing.py:1249` correctly gates on those classes, so class E / A-II juvenile offenders get adult sentencing — which is legally correct (§70.05 doesn't cover them), but no note explains why a juvenile is getting an adult sentence. Defensive: add an `ExceptionPath` or note in the output when a juvenile offender is sentenced outside §70.05.

#### Youthful offenders (PL 60.02 / CPL 720.10) — YO substitution not modeled
**Scope note:** in scope — YO status (when granted by court) changes sentence output based on facts we have (age category, offense, classification). Court's eligibility determination and redetermination procedure are out of scope.

Current state: `AgeCategory.YOUTHFUL_OFFENDER_ELIGIBLE` exists in `models.py:43` but **does nothing in the resolver**. grep confirms no code checks this value to change sentence output. The UI collects it (`sentencing_ui.py:277`) but the resolver ignores it entirely. A 17-year-old convicted of a class B felony with `YOUTHFUL_OFFENDER_ELIGIBLE` set gets the same sentence as a 30-year-old.

**The core gap (PL 60.02(2)):** when the court grants YO status, a felony is sentenced as **class E felony** regardless of original class. This is a sentence-output change comparable in magnitude to the hate crime bump — a class B felony becomes class E for sentencing purposes. Not modeled.

**Implementation needs:**
- [ ] **#YO1. Model YO adjudication as a sentence modifier.** Need a `case_facts.yo_adjudication_granted` boolean (court determination, NOT automatic from age category). When `yo_adjudication_granted == True AND age_category == YOUTHFUL_OFFENDER_ELIGIBLE`, substitute the effective classification with `ClassE_Felony` for sentencing purposes — analogous to how the SMF/hate crime/terrorism modifiers bump classification at `sentencing.py:1214-1234`. Re-run VFO check on the new classification (class E felony is generally non-VFO unless offense-specific). Re-run routing logic with the new classification. NB: this is a *downgrade* (B→E) rather than an *upgrade* like hate crime/terrorism — the modifier order at `sentencing.py:1214` may need to be adjusted, or YO may need to be applied as a pre-routing substitution.
- [ ] **#YO2. Art 220 CD/UD gate within YO (§60.02(2) proviso).** When YO is granted for an Article 220 drug felony, conditional discharge and unconditional discharge are NOT available as alternatives. Currently `_generate_felony_alternatives` at `sentencing.py:703-728` emits CD and UD for all non-class-A felonies. Fix: gate CD/UD emission on `not (yo_adjudication_granted AND is_drug_offense)`.
- [ ] **#YO3. YO finding cannot serve as predicate.** Per treatise and case law: a YO finding "is not a judgment of conviction" and cannot be used to establish predicate felon status. Currently if a user selects `SECOND_FELONY_OFFENDER_70_06` with `age_category == YOUTHFUL_OFFENDER_ELIGIBLE`, the system would apply §70.06 ranges — but if the prior was a YO adjudication, this is legally wrong. Fix: either (a) add a `prior_was_yo_finding` boolean on `CaseFacts` and gate predicate status on it, or (b) document that the user is responsible for not entering a YO prior as a predicate (consistent with our general approach of treating prior-record status as user input).
- [ ] **#YO4. 4-year aggregate cap on consecutive YO sentences (case law).** Consecutive YO prison terms cannot have aggregate max > 4 years (except violation of probation cases). Needs the same `CaseFacts.concurrent_offenses` field already needed for fines #4 and surcharges #S3. Fix: when `yo_adjudication_granted AND concurrent_offenses AND aggregate_max > 4`, flag the cap.
- [ ] **#YO5. Prostitution/loitering special case (§60.02(4)).** When YO finding is substituted for prostitution or loitering for prostitution (NOT patronizing) committed at age 16-17, court must impose a violation-level sentence + may order specialized services per Social Services Law title 8-A. Offense-specific + age-specific gate. Fix: gate on `section in prostitution_sections AND age at offense in (16, 17)`.

**Cross-reference:**
- [ ] **#YO6. YO surcharge/fee exemption — already logged as #S4.** Post-2020 repeal of §§60.02(3) and 60.35(10): YOs no longer pay mandatory surcharge or CVAF. Already noted under surcharges.

**Out of scope:**
- YO eligibility determination (CPL §720.10(2) exclusions: class A-I/A-II felony, armed felony, rape 1st, criminal sexual act 1st, aggravated sexual abuse; §720.10(3) mitigating-circumstances exception for armed felony) — court determination, not sentence output. User is responsible for selecting `yo_adjudication_granted` only when eligible.
- §720.20(5) redetermination procedure (5-year wait, 9-factor test, DA objection) — procedural, separate flow.

### Key architecture decisions
- **JSON is canonical** — `data/lists.json` + `data/sentencing_tables.json` are the source of truth. SQLite DB is outdated and ignored.
- **Modifiers applied in order**: SMF → hate crime → terrorism. Each sees the previous modifier's result.
- **Prior-record-status normalization**: VFOs with non-VFO statuses fall back to 70.02 first offender; non-VFOs with VFO statuses fall back to 70.06/70.10 (then 70.06→first offender for A-I/A-II per 70.06(6)).
- **Offense-specific overrides** in `sentencing_tables.json` `offenseOverrides` section handle §125.27, §125.26, §130.95, §130.96.
